1) As encrypted DNS, ECH, VPNs, and other privacy technologies make selective traffic classification increasingly difficult, what happens to a censor’s incentives? 
One possibility is that censorship becomes less precise but more disruptive; since it’s unable to identify precisely, a censor may resort to blocking more IP ranges, protocols, VPNs, or infrastructure providers etc.
Interesting, an opposite story is if these technologies create enough collateral damage that an attempt to block is doomed to disrupt economically or socially essential services, could this effectively raise the cost of censorship and thus deter blocking behavior? Essentially an "one goes down, everything goes down” threat
Both dynamics seem plausible, I’m curious if different equilibrium would emerge in different countries/network environments.

2) How does selective throttling interact with TCP congestion control? A traffic shaper can deliberately drop packets from a targeted queue, causing TCP to interpret the losses as congestion and reduce its sending rate. How would the observable behavior differ if the censor instead directly limited the queue’s service rate without dropping packets? Could measurements of packet loss, congestion-window behavior, and throughput distinguish these implementations (and how much would this cost in general), or could a very careful censor make throttling technically/genuinely indistinguishable from ordinary congestion?

3) SYN cookies avoid allocating server state during the first stage of the TCP handshake, while other defenses such as stateful filtering must maintain info about previous packets or requests. The attacker could, potentially, deliberately shift its target from the original server resource to the firewall or other defensive infrastructure. Can DoS defenses in this case reproduce the same resource asymmetry they are designed to eliminate, and how could we design a defense that ensures the defender never performs substantially more computation or state allocation than the attacker must expend to trigger it?
